Play safely / 04

Prepare well.
Keep enjoying it.

Good preparation starts before the first rally. Choose suitable equipment and a safe place to play, warm up, progress gradually, and give yourself time to recover.

01 / Before the first rally

Set yourself up for a good session

✓Choose suitable shoes. Wear well-fitting indoor court shoes with venue-approved, non-marking soles and reliable grip. Read the shoe-fitting guide.

✓Check your equipment. Use a racket that feels manageable and check the playing area for loose items or hazards. Read the equipment guide.

✓Pick a safe venue. Look for a clear, adequately lit court with enough space around it. Consider conditions such as wind if playing outdoors.

✓Play with a willing partner. Agree on the pace and type of practice so both players can take part comfortably.

02 / Ease into movement

Warm up before you speed up

Use a progressive warm-up as a bridge from rest to play, not a fitness test. Begin with easy movement, then rehearse court-relevant actions at a comfortable range and pace. Finish with a few gentle hits before faster rallies.

  1. Raise temperature gently: walk, jog easily, or move comfortably around the available space.
  2. Mobilize dynamically: use comfortable ankle movements, calf raises, gentle hip and shoulder rotations, and easy reaching; avoid forcing a stretch.
  3. Rehearse the game: practise side steps, controlled split steps, lunges within range, and relaxed racket swings.
  4. Build pace gradually: start with short gentle rallies and increase speed only if movement feels controlled.

There is no required routine that fits everyone. Modify or skip movements that cause pain, and ask a qualified professional for an individualized plan if health or injury affects your exercise.

Make it your own: Keep movements comfortable and controlled. If you have a condition or injury that affects exercise, ask a qualified professional how to adapt your warm-up.

03 / Sustainable training

Build up gradually

Choose a practice plan that fits your current ability, schedule, and recovery. Increase one aspect at a time—such as session length, intensity, or number of sessions—and notice how you feel afterward. More training is not automatically more effective.

  • Use technique practice and cooperative rallies when learning new movements.
  • Take breaks before fatigue makes movement feel uncontrolled.
  • Include lighter sessions and rest days in your weekly plan.
  • Adjust your session if you feel unusually tired or uncomfortable.

04 / Between sessions

Recovery is part of practice

Leave time between harder sessions, get adequate sleep, and drink according to your needs and conditions. Gentle cool-down movement can help you transition out of play. A short training note can help you spot when a session felt too demanding and adjust the next one.

Return to training at a comfortable level after a break rather than immediately resuming your hardest workload.

05 / Listen to your body

Pause when something feels wrong

Stop playing if you feel unwell, dizzy, or injured. Do not try to play through significant pain. Seek advice from a qualified health professional when symptoms are concerning, persistent, or you are unsure whether it is safe to continue.

Important: This page provides general educational information, not personalized medical or injury-prevention advice. It cannot replace assessment or guidance from a qualified professional.
